Core Concepts

Armies

Squads

Armies in Bloodbloom are broken down into squads. A squad represents a single coherent fighting force which acts as one during the course of the battle. Squads are selected for activation together and every model within a squad must take the same Action.

Squads and models from your army are considered friendly; squads and models from another player's army are opposing.

Squads cost Supply to deploy and maintain into any theatre of war, generally placing a limit to how many you can field in your army.

Models

Models are the individual troops, tanks, and commanders that make up your army. Every model in your army will be in a squad, though this may be a squad of one in some cases.

Models are prescribed a set of characteristics from their statsheets. These unmodified values are referred to as the model’s attributes. There are many factors that can modify a given model’s characteristics, so the attributes including any modifications are referred to as the model’s values.

When a rule refers to a model's characteristic, it will generally be referring to that model's trait value, unless specified otherwise.

Example
A model may have a Movement attribute of 6″, but including a Wound Marker penalty it has a Movement value of 4″.

Penalties to Aim, Prowess, and Leadership values increase the threshold (e.g. a −2 penalty to a 3+ Aim results in a 5+ Aim). Bonuses decrease it. Aim and Prowess thresholds can never be increased to below 2+, and Movement can never be reduced below 0″.

Tags
Models have tags that help classify them into different groups or categories, such as Infantry. On their own, tags do nothing, but other rules and abilities may reference specific tags to determine their effects.

Heroes

Heroes are the champions, leaders, and specialist operatives of your armies. Generally a hero will be a squad containing a single power model, usually with unique abilities that can shape a battlefield.

Heroes blur the line of squads, going where they please, when they please. During the Movement Phase of a combat round, a Hero may join with a squad to lead it, this is done by moving the Hero to within Close-Combat Range of a friendly squad. A Hero can only ever be within Close-Combat Range of a single squad, movement that would put a Hero within Close-Combat Range of two or more friendly squads is considered invalid.

While a Hero is leading a squad, it is considered part of the squad, it uses the squad’s attributes, and shares the squad’s abilities and tags (but not traits). It can be allocated Wound Markers, but if it is ever destroyed then it counts as having been a destroyed squad and no longer shares abilities with the squad it was leading. Likewise if every model of a squad other than the Hero is destroyed, then it too counts as having been a destroyed squad and the hero no longer benefits from the squad’s abilities.

Note
If a Hero is leading a squad, add a +2 bonus to any Command Tests the squad makes.

Reinforcements

Commanders loathe to commit all of their forces to a single theatre, and generally keep a backup of reserves to be strategically deployed as required.

Your reinforcements are squads that remain un-deployed to the side of the board. They can be brought in during the Reinforcement Step of the Deployment Phase, via the Deploy action. Generally, there are no limit to how many of your squads can be in your reinforcements.

Squads in your reinforcements can not be the target of effects or actions other than Deploy.

Requisitions

As a battle proceeds, commanders may see fit to employ more troops to a theatre to replace losses.

Each player has a Requisition Limit equal to half of their armies Supply Limit. Whenever one of their squads is destroyed, the controlling player may opt to use up some of their available Requisitions, equal to the squad's Supply Cost, to add the destroyed squad into their Reinforcements, so long as doing so would not cause them to exceed their Requisition Limit.

Cohesion

A squad is considered Cohesive if every model is horizontally within 1″ of at least one other model in the squad and it is possible to trace a chain of models, each within 1″ of the next, connecting any two models in the squad.

Locked-in-Combat

A squad is Locked-in-Combat with another squad if any of its models are within Close-Combat Range of an opposing model in that squad.

Close-Combat Range

A model is within Close-Combat Range of another if it is within 1” horizontally of an opposing model, measured from one base to another, and if the height difference between the models bases is no more than 5”.

Line of Sight

Line of sight refers to a model’s ability to see another model. This is generally done by assuming the position of the model’s head and looking out to gauge whether or not it could see the other model. If the model does not have an obvious head, then the geometric center of the model may be used instead.

In order for a model to see another, at least 25% of the other model, or its base, must be visible to the viewing model.

Models can see through other models in their squad, but may not see through models in another squad, friendly or otherwise, unless the target model can still see 25% of the target model regardless of the obstruction.

Cover

A targeted squad is said to have Cover against a given model if the majority (greater than or equal to 50%) of the models in the targeted squad each have at least 25% of their model obscured by terrain.

If a squad has Cover against an attacking model, then Ranged Attacks made against that squad by the model are made with a -1 penalty to hit.

Terrain

Terrain is any piece of the battlefield that is not a model. Terrain can be used for cover, to block line of sight, and to provide strategic advantages. Terrain can be natural, such as a tree or a hill, or it can be artifical such as a building or a barricade.

As described by the cover section, any terrain that blocks at least 25% of a model from an attacker may provide a -1 to hit penality.

Terrain Area

Some pieces of terrain, such as forrests or buildings, come with bases that describe the area that the terrain should occupy. While a model is wholly within the area of a piece of terrain it is always considered to be obscured for the purposes cover against any attacker not also wholly within that piece of terrain.

Objectives

A match of Bloodbloom is played until one player has achieved enough Victory Points to win the match. Victory Points are generally gained by completing Objectives. There are two main types of Objectives: Battlefield Objectives & Agendas.

Battlefield Objectives

While each army may have their own specific reasons for engaging on this field of battle, there will ultimately be a shared set of goals that the forces are vying for. These are Battlefield Objectives, shared by both armies.

A Battlefield Objective may be Progressive or Tactical. Tactical Objectives may only be scored once by each army, allocating a set amount of Victory Points to an army that scores it. Progressive Objectives will continue to give Victory Points to an army that is able to complete its requirements.

At the beginning of a match, only a single Battlefield Objective is revealed, likely the reason that the armies have come to engage in the first place. As the battle progresses, new Objectives become relevant. Each round, at the end of the Strategic Phase, a new Battlefield Objective is revealed for the armies to engage over.

Generally a match will have four Battlefield Objectives, with the first army to reach 10 Victory Points winning.

Agendas

Unbenounced to your opponent are the hidden reasons your army has chosen to fight here. These hidden objectives are your Agendas. Like Battlefield Objectives, scoring these will gain you Victory Points, and each Agenda can only be scored once. Agendas are hidden information, only revealed to your opponent when they are scored.

Orders

Well timed orders can turn the tide of battle, inspiring soldiers to go above and beyond their call of duty. Orders are special actions that the Players make. There is no limit to the number of times an Order can be played, but Players do have a finite amount of Order Points (OP) to spend each Round.

Order Points

Order Points are gained each round based on the number of strategic squads still standing on the battlefield. These squads tend to be less effective at direct combat but offer the player advantage through being able to perform more tactical orders.

At the start of each round, you gain 1 Order Point. At any given time you may have up to 5 Order Points, any generated past this are lost.

Standard Orders

The following Orders are available to all players regardless of faction.

Reroll 1 OP
Phase: Any Phase

After any single test of dice has been rolled by you, reroll all dice and take the new result.

Note
A given test generally refers to a single set of dice rolled for a specific purpose, such as an attack or a command test. If you roll multiple tests together, such as rolling all of the attacks for a squad, you must choose which one of the tests to reroll when using this Order.
Sprint 1 OP
Phase: Movement Phase

When a squad starts a movement, roll a d6 and add the result to their movement value. May only be used once per squad per phase.

Sequence Break 2 OP
Phase: Movement or Engagement Phase

After a friendly squad has finished their action but before you end your turn, you may activate another squad.

Bravery 2 OP
Phase: Any Phase

Before a squad takes a Command Test, they automatically pass instead.

Update Directives 3 OP
Phase: Strategic Phase (Agenda Step)

Discard an unscored Agenda and draw a new one.

Ready 4 OP
Phase: Engagement Phase

Before activating a squad on your turn, remove the Activation Token from an already-activated squad to ready them.

Activations

When a squad is selected for activation during the Activation Step of a phase, then they are considered to be activated until the end of the phase (or if any rule would ready them before then).

Once selected for activation, the squad must be assigned a relevant action from the phase (or other valid source) that they meet the requirements for. An Activation Token should be placed next to the squad after it has concluded its selected Action, unless told otherwise.

An activated squad may never be selected again for activation until it has first removed its current Activation Token and thus been readied.

Instant Actions

Instant Actions are the most straightforward type of action, generally having view requirements and being resolved immediately.

Prepare Actions

Unlike Instant Actions, Prepare Actions generally do not resolve their effect immediately, but instead set a squad up to complete something at a later point in the round. This will often be to their advantage if what they prepare for comes to fruition.

Prepare Actions will often come with a Reminder Marker that marks the squad as activated but can then be replaced with an Activation Token when the action is resolved.

Interruptible

Many Prepare Actions require concentration from the squad and are therefore interruptible by the opponent. If a squad is performing an Interruptible Prepare Action and they are targeted by a Ranged Attack, then after the attack is concluded, the squad must make a Command Test with a -1 penalty for each Hit that was scored against them (Note that the hit does not necessarily have to produce a Hit Marker). If they fail this, or the activated squad ever becomes Locked-in-Combat, replace their relevant Reminder Marker with an Activation Token.

Reactions

Reactions can only be done in response to a trigger. A reaction is different from the other two action types as they can be declared on another player’s turn. Reactions have two additional descriptions, a trigger and a timing.

The trigger specifies when the reaction may be declared, generally it will be after an opposing squad does something, in which case that squad is the triggering squad. Generally, if you do something that may give your opponent an opportunity to use a Reaction, you should give them time to declare it.

The timing specifies when the reaction should be resolved. Generally, this will be immediately after the triggering Action has been concluded, but sometimes it may vary. Once the Reaction has been declared, the squad is considered activated even before the Reaction is resolved (and as such can not be used for another Reaction).

Movement

When a model is instructed to move, you may move it in a series of unobstructed straight lines. A model may not move through terrain or other models that are not in its squad unless otherwise specified.

Pivots

Models that do not have circular bases may rotate during their movement, however each rotation lowers their Movement value by 1” for the duration of that movement. Rotations are always about the geometric center of a model’s base.

Flying

Models with the Flying trait may move through the air, so long as they finish their movement on a surface that can entirely fit their base. When moving through the air, measure the direct 3-dimensional movement of the model through the air.

Obstacles

Terrains vertically smaller than 2” tall are considered Obstacles, and may be moved through by models at the cost of lowering their Movement value by 2” for the duration of that movement.

Terrain larger than 2” can not be moved through, though it may be climbed by models with the Infantry tag. For a model to climb a piece of vertical terrain, it must have enough movement to traverse the vertical distance, in addition to any horizontal movement required so that its entire base can fit onto the new surface. If the surface does not have enough room for the entire base of the model to fit, then the model may not climb that terrain.

When flying up to a higher surface, the model should measure its 3-dimensional movement to the edge of the surface, then whatever horizontal movement is required so that the model can fit its entire base on the surface.

Command Test

When a squad is instructed to make a Command Test, you must roll 3d6 and compare the result to its Leadership value, adding any bonuses or penalties. If the final result is larger than or equal to the squad’s Leadership value, then the squad passes the Command Test, otherwise, it fails.

Modifier Effect
Unwounded Sergeant in squad +1 to Command Tests
Hero leading the squad +2 to Command Tests

Playing The Match

Rounds

Rounds in Bloombloom are played through in sequential phases. Phases are often into step Activations, and Resolutions. Starting with the Activation Step, players take alternating turns activating their squads, beginning with the player with the Initiative Token. In each phase, unless told otherwise, players activate a single squad and resolve an Action before then letting the turn pass to the other player.

Players continue alternatively activating squads until they have no more unactivated squads available, in which case, a player with unactivated squads available may continue sequentially activating them until they too have no more unactivated squads available.

Once both players have activated all of their squads, the phase moves into the Resolution Step, where any unresolved outcomes (Such as Hit Markers) of the previous step may be applied. Once all unresolved outcomes have been settled, players may ready their squads by removing their Activation Tokens and proceeding to the next phase. If, at the end of a round, no player has won yet, then a new round begins starting from the Movement Phase.

Movement Phase

During the Movement Phase, players seek to position their squads around the battlefield in order to out manoeuvre their opponent.

Activation Step

During this step of the Movement Phase, the following Actions are available:

Hold Fast Instant

The activated squad makes no movement and each model remains in place.

Move Instant
Requirements: Squad may not be Locked-in-Combat.

Each model in the activated squad may make a movement up to its movement value in inches. They may not pass, nor end this movement, within Close-Combat range of an opposing squad.

Regroup Instant
Requirements: The activated squad must be Locked-in-Combat.

The squad must take a Command Test. Failing this, the squad is unable to move. If they succeed the Command Test, then each model in the squad must make a movement up to its movement value in inches, ending its movement outside of Close-Combat range of any opposing squad.

Pounce Reaction
Trigger: Another player Regroups or Routes a squad that is Locked-in-Combat one or more of your unactivated squads.
Timing: After the opposing squad has taken any Command Test, but before moving (or not moving) any models.

Your Locked-in-Combat squad makes a Melee Opportunity Attack against the triggering squad.

Note
There is no limit to how many squads may take the Pounce action per an opposing Regroup or Route. If a squad triggers this reaction while being Locked-in-Combat with two opposing squads, then both opposing squads may be selected to Pounce. Though both must be selected before resolving any attack.

Resolution Step

Any Hit Markers generated during this phase may now be allocated and models destroyed. This can be done simultaneously, or following an alternating squad resolution starting with the player with the Initiative Token if the order matters.

Deployment Phase

In the Deployment Phase, reinforcements flood into the battlefield and troops ready themselves into or from transports. Squads are not activated in this phase and players take turns going through the Embarking Step then the Reinforcing Step.

Embarking Step

During this step, players alternate declaring if they will Embark or Disembark any squads from transports. If on a player’s turn, they decline to either Embark or Disembark, then they are considered to have passed for this step. Once both players are passed move onto the next step.

Transports

Models with the Transport trait can allow other models to be stored inside them. During this time, those models can not be targeted with actions or abilities, but may make Ranged Attacks under certain conditions.

A given transport will have a specific tag (or tags) and a capacity. The transport may only contain a number of models whose combined sizes add up to its capacity. A model can only Embark into a transport if it has the tag specified and if their entire squad also Embarks.

A given squad can either Embark or Disembark in the Embarking Step of the Deployment Phase, but not both.

Embark Instant
Requirements: Every model within a squad must be wholly within 3” of a target transport and the squad may not be Locked-in-Combat.

Remove all models, and any of their Markers, from the board and place them aside. They are now within the target transport.

Disembark Instant
Requirements: The activating squad is currently within a given transport.

Place all models from the activated squad wholly within 3” of the transport. If any models can not be placed wholly within 3" for whatever reason, they are destroyed instead. After placing all models of a squad, if you had placed any models into Close-Combat range of opposing models, those models may make a Melee Opportunity Attack against the placed squad.

Transport Destroyed
If a transport is destroyed while carrying squads, before removing the transport model from the board, Disembark any squads and roll a 1d3 for each model within those squads. On a result of 2 or less, a Wound Marker is generated for that squad.

Reinforcing Step

During this step, players alternate declaring if they will Deploy any squads from their reinforcements. If on a player’s turn, they decline to Deploy a squad from their reinforcements, then they are considered to have passed for this step. Once both players are passed move onto the next phase.

Resolution Step

Any Hit Markers generated during this phase may now be allocated and models destroyed. This can be done simultaneously, or following an alternating squad resolution starting with the player with the Initiative Token if the order matters.

Engagement Phase

During the Engagement Phase, players seek to battle for tactical superiority whether that be through well timed volleys or brazen charges.

Activation Step

During this step of the Engagement Phase, the following Actions are available to an unactivated squad:

Fire! Instant
Requirements: The activated squad may not be Locked-in-Combat and must have at least one eligible ranged weapon.

Each model in the activated squad may perform a Ranged Attack.

Strike! Instant
Requirements: The activated squad must be Locked-in-Combat and must have at least one melee weapon.

Each model in the activated squad may perform a Melee Attack.

Dash Instant
Requirements: The activated squad may not be Locked-in-Combat.
Limit: Only one Melee Attack per squad can be performed as the result of this action.

Each model in the activated squad may make a movement up to its movement value in inches. If the activated squad moves into Close-Combat range with an opposing squad, they may make a Melee Attack with a +1 to hit bonus.

Ready Blades Prepare (Interruptible)
Requirements: The activated squad must be Locked-in-Combat and must have at least one melee weapon.

A Readied Marker is placed next to the activated squad, this marker functions as an Activation Token and the squad counts as being activated this step. While the squad has this Readied Marker, if it ever becomes Locked-in-Combat with an opposing squad, it may replace the Readied Marker with an Activation Token and then take the Strike! action with a +1 to hit bonus.

Note
Being Locked-in-Combat does not prevent this from being used (as would normally be in case with an Interruptible action), so long as it is used immediately after entering Locked-in-Combat.
Overwatch Prepare (Interruptible)
Requirements: The activated squad may not be Locked-in-Combat and must have at least one ranged weapon.

An Overwatch Marker is placed next to the activated squad, this marker functions as an Activation Token and the squad counts as being activated this step. While the squad has this Overwatch Marker, at any point before the end of the phase, the squad may also take the Snapshot reaction, though it makes a normal Ranged Attack instead of a Ranged Opportunity Attack, while resolving that reaction. If this reaction is resolved, the Overwatch Marker is replaced with an Activation Token.

Note
The Snapshot reaction may be made even if the triggering squad’s movement results in the activated squad becoming Locked-in-Combat. See the Snapshot reaction’s notes for more details.
Retreat Prepare (Interruptible)
Requirements: The activated squad must be wholly within your deployment area.

A Retreat Marker is placed next to the activated squad, this marker functions as an Activation Token and the squad counts as being activated at this step. At the end of the Resolution Step of this phase, if this squad is still wholly within your deployment area, you may remove any remaining models. You may add these models as a new squad into your reinforcements and remove any Wound Markers (or any other status markers they had).

Snapshot Reaction
Trigger: An opposing squad moves within line of sight of one of your unactivated squads.
Requirements: The activated squad may not be Locked-in-Combat.
Timing: After the triggering squad has finished its movement.

Each model in the activated squad may perform a Ranged Opportunity Attack against the triggering squad. If the result of the movement means that no model is eligible to perform the attack, then the action is refunded; the activating squad does not count as being activated.

Note
The Opportunity Attack assumes that the attack is made at the point in time that benefits the attacker most. If the triggering squad ends its movement out of sight or in cover with respect to the activated squad, then the attacks can be assumed to have been made at an earlier point in the triggering squad’s movement such that they were in line of sight and out of cover with respect to the activated squad (though only if such a point exists).

Additionally, a Snapshot may still be declared if the triggering squad’s movement results in the activated squad becoming Locked-in-Combat. This is because the Snapshot reaction is made upon the activated squad sighting the movement, even if the attacks themselves are not resolved until after the triggering squad’s movement.
Defend Reaction
Trigger: An opposing squad Dashes into Close-Combat range with one of your unactivated squads.
Timing: After the opposing squad has concluded their movement but before a Melee Attack is performed.

For the duration of the phase, if the triggering squad performs a Melee Attack against the activated squad, they do so with a -2 to hit penalty.

Hunker Down Reaction
Trigger: An opposing squad targets one of your unactivated squads with a Ranged Attack.
Requirements: The activated squad may not be Locked-in-Combat.
Timing: After the opposing squad has finished declaring targets but before a Ranged Attack is performed.

For the duration of the phase, if the activated squad has cover against an opposing model who is making a Ranged Attack, then the opposing model gains a -1 to hit ranged penalty, in addition to that provided by the cover.

Resolution Step

Any Hit Markers generated during this phase may now be allocated and models destroyed. This can be done simultaneously, or following an alternating squad resolution starting with the player with the Initiative Token if the order matters.

Once this has been resolved, other Actions such as Retreat or Objective-specific Actions may be resolved.

Leadership Phase

After a grueling round of manoeuvring and bloodshed, commanders and sergeants alike attempt to rally their troops to remain in the carnage.

Morale Step

During the Morale Step, players will need to take Command Tests for all squads in their army who have taken significant casualties.

If a squad has gained any Wound Markers over the course of this round, then compare the number of unwounded models to the number of wounds generated this round. If the number of Wounds Markers generated is equal to or larger than the number of unwounded models, the squad must make a Command Test, with a penalty equal to the difference between Wound Markers generated and unwounded models.

If the squad fails the Command Test then they are considered Routing. They gain a new Action: Rout, usable in the Movement and Engagement Phase, and no other Action can be taken in any phase until they are no longer Routing. You may not score objectives, Battlefield or Agenda, with Routing units.

Rout Instant
Requirements: Squad must be Routing.

Each model in the squad must make a movement of its movement value in inches, taking the shortest path towards the board’s edge in the controlling player’s deployment area. No model in the squad may come within Close-Combat range of an opposing squad after starting this movement. If this is not possible, remove the model.

After finishing this movement, if the squad is wholly within the controlling player’s deployment area, remove the squad - it is destroyed. Otherwise, the squad makes a Command Test. If it passes, the squad is no longer Routing.

Disband Step

During this step, players may declare if any of their Heroes, who are leading squads, will depart from those squads. If this is the case, a Hero may move up to 1” and must end that movement out of Close-Combat range of any friendly squads, and may not enter into Close-Combat range of any opposing squads that it was not already Locked-in-Combat against at the start of this step.

Strategic Phase

New strategic orders come in from on high, maps are marked and commanders given bottom-lines.

Agenda Step

During this step, players may declare if they have scored any Agendas this Round.

Resolution Step

During this step, if there are any unrevealed Battlefield Objectives remaining, reveal the next objectives and resolve any of its requirements. The Initiative Token is given to the other player.

Ending the Match

The match ends upon a player reaching 10 Victory Points, with that player being the victor of the match. If two players reach 10 Victory Points at the same time, then the match continues until the next time a player would gain Victory Points - if this too is simultaneous then the match is a draw.

Combat

Ranged Combat

When a model is selected to make a Ranged Attack, it selects a single ranged weapon that it is equipped with, and selects a target opposing squad that is both visible to the model and within the selected weapon’s range. It then performs an attack using the Combat Sequence below.

Melee Combat

When a model is selected to make a Melee Attack, if it is not already in Close-Combat range with an opposing model it may first move up to 3” towards one. It then selects a single melee weapon that it is equipped with, and selects a target opposing squad that is both visible to the model and within Close-Combat range. It then performs an attack using the Combat Sequence below.

If a model does not have any melee weapons equipped, then it may select the following weapon:

Name Range Attacks Pierce Rating Stopping Power
Improvised Attack Melee 1 0 1

When selecting a melee weapon, the model may choose to make Sweeping Attacks. If this is done, double the number of attacks the weapon makes but lower its Penetration and Stopping Powers by 1 step for the duration of this attack.

Opportunity Attacks

Attacks of opportunity are made similarly to other attacks but carry an innate -2 penalty to hit due to their hasty nature. Ranged Opportunity Attacks, such as when taking the Snapshot reaction, are assumed to be taken at the most beneficial moment for the attacker. If the target squad is Dashing and would necessarily have a moment where the majority of models in the squad do not have cover against the attacking model, then this is assumed to be the case when resolving the Opportunity Attack.

Combat Sequence — Activation Step

Rolling to Hit

The model rolls a 1d6 for the attack and adds any relevant bonuses or penalties. If the result of the roll plus any modifiers, is above or equal to their To Hit value, then a hit is scored and proceeds to the next step in the sequence.

The model’s To Hit value is their Aim value for a Ranged Attack and their Prowess value for a Melee Attack.

This value can be modified past 7+ (Making it impossible to hit) but can not be modified below 2+ (A roll of ‘1’ will always miss).

Modifier Value
Melee Attack after Dashing into combat +1 to hit
Melee Attack against opponents Defending −2 to hit
Ranged Attack against squad in Cover −1 to hit
Ranged Attack against squad Hunkering Down in Cover −2 to hit
Any Opportunity Attack −2 to hit

Firing into Melee

A squad making a Ranged Attack, whose models select a target opposing squad which is Locked-in-Combat with a friendly squad, must first take a Command Test. Failing this they must choose another eligible target for their attacks, this new target may not be Locked-in-Combat with any friendly squads. If no such target exists, then the squad instead ends their activation without making any attacks.

If the Command Test passes, then the squad may proceed to roll to hit. After determining the number of hits scored, compare the total number of friendly models Locked-in-Combat with the target squad to the number of models in the target squad, then roll 1d6 for each hit and consult the table below:

Relative Numbers Friendly Hit Range
Target squad has at least 3 times as many models as the friendly models 1
Target squad has more models than the friendly models, but fewer than 3 times as many 1–2
Target squad and the friendly models have the same number of models 1–3
Friendly models have more models than the target squad, but fewer than 3 times as many 1–4
Friendly models have at least 3 times as many models as the target squad 1–5

If a roll falls within the relevant ‘Friendly Hit Range’ then the scored hit must be allocated to a friendly squad Locked-in-Combat with the target squad, and resolved as normal. Otherwise, it is allocated to the target squad.

A squad gains a -1 penalty to Command Tests for the remainder of the round for every Hit Marker generated against them from friendly squads firing into melee.

Rolling against Armour

Once a hit has been successfully scored against an opposing squad, another roll is required to determine if it is able to breach the armour of the foe.

Compare the Pierce Rating value of the selected weapon against the Armour Rating value of the targeted squad using the rules below:

  • If the Pierce Rating exceeds or equals the Armour Rating, then the attack automatically succeeds.
  • If the Pierce Rating is less than the Armour Rating, then for each successful hit, roll a 1d6, if the result is larger than or equal to the relevant threshold below, then the attack succeeds.
PP vs AR Success Threshold
Pierce Rating is 1 step less than Armour Rating 4+
Pierce Rating is 2 steps less than Armour Rating 5+
Pierce Rating is 3 steps less than Armour Rating 6+
Pierce Rating is 4 or more steps less than Armour Rating Cannot succeed

Finally, if the attack succeeds, but the Pierce Rating is equal to or less than the Armour Rating, then the Stopping Power value of the attack is lowered by 1.

Otherwise, for every 2 steps that the Pierce Rating exceeds the Armour Rating, increase the Stopping Power value of the attack by 1.

Each successful attack results in a Hit Marker being generated against the target squad. Hit Markers are best represented by dice whose value is set to the Stopping Power value of the attack. There is no limit to how many Hit Markers can be generated against a given squad.

Once an attack has either failed or generated a hit, the attack sequence ends.

Combat Sequence — Resolution Step

For every Hit Marker against a squad, the squad’s controlling player must roll to determine the damage done to models in that squad. Hit Markers against a squad can be allocated in any order, one at a time, to individual models within that squad by the controlling player.

When an attack is allocated to a model, you compare the Stopping Power value of the attack to the Toughness value of the model then roll a 2d3 to determine the outcome based on the following table:

Relative Values Wound Threshold
Stopping Power is 3 or more steps below Toughness Cannot wound
Stopping Power is 2 steps below Toughness 6+
Stopping Power is 1 step below Toughness 5+
Stopping Power is equal to Toughness 4+
Stopping Power exceeds the Toughness by 1 step 3+
Stopping Power exceeds the Toughness by 2 or more steps The attack automatically wounds the model.

If a wound is generated, then a Wound Marker is placed against the allocated model. If a model ever has as many (or more) Wound Markers as their Endurance value, then the model is destroyed and removed from the squad.

A successful wound places a Wound Marker on the model. When Wound Markers ≥ Endurance the model is destroyed. Each Wound Marker applies:

  • −1 to Aim and Prowess values (stacking).
  • −2″ to Movement value (stacking).

Once all Wound Markers have been applied and models destroyed, the controlling player must check to see if the squad is cohesive, if it is not they must remove models from the squad until the squad is cohesive.

Reference — Squad Traits

Sentinel X

A squad with this trait may make an additional X Snapshot reactions when taking the Overwatch action before replacing the Overwatch Marker with an Activation Token. (It is recommended that X+1 Overwatch Markers be placed next to the squad, with one being removed after each Snapshot).

Guard X″

After an opposing squad finishes a movement within X inches of a squad with this trait that has taken the Ready Blades action (And still has the Readied Marker), if this squad is not Locked-in-Combat, you may move each model up to X inches, so long as at least one model finishes its movement within Close-Combat range of the opposing squad.

Reference — Model Traits

War Machine

This model does not suffer the standard penalties from gaining Wound Markers. Instead, whenever a Wound Marker is assigned to the model, roll a d6 and add the Stopping Power of the Attack minus the Toughness of the model to the result, then compare that to the table below.

Transport X Y…

Models with the transport trait can have up to X worth of size of models Embarked within them, so long as they have at least one of the tags listed in Y…

Hover

Models with the hover trait may pass over Obstacles without taking the penalty to their movement value.

Jump X″

Models with the jump trait may ignore up to X inches of vertical terrain movement when climbing.

Reference — Weapon Traits

Pinpoint

When allocating Hit Markers generated from attacks with this weapon, the attacking player decides which model must resolve the wound instead of the controlling player.

Note
The controlling player still decides the order in which Hit Markers are allocated.
It may help to use different coloured Hit Markers for these attacks.
Mounted

This weapon may also be selected to make a Ranged Attack when the bearer selects a ranged weapon to attack with. It may target the same or different squad as the other ranged weapon.

Coaxial

This weapon may also be selected to make a Ranged Attack when the bearer selects a ranged weapon to attack with. It must target the same squad as the other ranged weapon.

Explosive X

When a hit is scored with this weapon, roll a d6 for each model in the opposing squad. On a roll of X or higher, produce an extra scored hit.

When Firing into Melee, also roll a d6 for each model in a friendly squad that is Locked-in-Combat with the target squad.

Flamer dX

When a hit is scored with this weapon, roll X and produce that many extra scored hits against the opposing squad.

Stun X

At the end of a player's turn, if a squad has received any number of Hit Markers from weapons with this trait, the controlling player rolls a D6. On a roll of X or higher, place an Activation Token next to the opposing squad. If the squad then has two or more Activation Tokens, only one is removed at the end of the phase; the squad will start the next Movement or Engagement phase activated.

A target squad can only gain an Activation Token from this trait once per round.

Mortar

Attacks with this weapon can target squads that the bearer does not have line of sight to, so long as at least one model in the controlling player’s army does have line of sight on that squad. Attacks made this way are at a -2 to hit penalty and the target squad is always presumed to have cover, though the penalty is already included in the -2 to hit.

Cull

Increase the Stopping Power of this weapons attack by 1 against targets with 1 or less Armour Rating.

Ignores Armour

Hits scored by this weapon automatically become Hit Markers.

Ignores Cover

Attacks made by this weapon do not suffer a penalty against targets in cover (or hunkered down).
